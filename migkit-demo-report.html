Methodology appendix

What was tested

These numbers describe scripted responses, not a real provider. At least one side of this comparison was produced by a Fake adapter (FakeAdapter for the baseline, FakeAdapter for the candidate). The only real thing in this document is the machinery: the sampling, the judging, the statistics and the decision rules are the production paths, exercised end to end. The quality difference they measure was written into the script.

5 draws per item over 12 items, giving 60 / 60 completions on the baseline (fake-baseline-v1) and 60 / 60 on the candidate (fake-candidate-v1), each shown as observed over expected. A migration decision needs a distribution per item rather than a single shot: one sample cannot separate 'the candidate is worse' from 'the candidate was unlucky once'.

1 judge(s) graded both sides: accuracy (fake-judge-v1, rubric cc39e4aad0ef5db8).

Item counts are reported in three states -- passing, failing and unstable -- and never as an item-level rate. An item passes at 80% or more of its draws and fails at 20% or less; between those it is unstable and is named rather than counted. A three-state classification does not reduce to one fraction without pushing the ambiguous items into one bucket or the other, and whichever bucket is chosen, the resulting number lies in that direction.

Why these tests

The pass rate is reported two ways because they answer different questions and are not interchangeable. The printed interval is the two-sided Wilson interval at 95.0% confidence, which is the range of rates compatible with what was observed. The gate uses the one-sided Wilson lower bound against a floor of 90.0%: the candidate must demonstrate that rate, not merely be consistent with it. A reader who conflates the two will believe the gate is looser than it is.

The regression test is the Mann-Whitney U statistic, one-sided with alternative='less' at alpha=0.0500, asking only whether the candidate is stochastically smaller than the baseline. It is deliberately one-tailed: an improvement is not a regression, and a two-tailed test would spend half its power looking for one. What actually ran on this evidence: mann-whitney-u.

Why nonparametric

Judge scores are a bounded 1-5 ordinal scale -- that is opik-rigor's own rubric contract, not a convention adopted here. The distance between 3 and 4 is not the distance between 4 and 5, so a t-test's interval-scale assumption is not merely unmet, it is unmeetable. Ranks are the only thing this data supports, which is why the comparison is a rank test rather than a comparison of means.

A completion that failed has no output and therefore no judge score. It is imputed at the rubric's minimum rather than dropped, because dropping it makes a model that crashes beat a model that answers badly: both post the same pass count, and the crasher's missing scores leave the rank test with nothing to notice.

What REVIEW means

REVIEW means the sample could not have detected the regression being asked about -- not that the pass-rate floor is unreachable. The configured minimum detectable effect is a 10.0% drop in pass rate at 80.0% power, and a sample that cannot reach that target yields REVIEW and never GO.

Judge accuracy: 60 completions per side observed against roughly 140 required for that effect at that power, so this judge is not powered for the question. The requirement comes from a two-proportion normal approximation, which is the right order of magnitude for the rank test that actually decides `regressed` rather than an exact figure for it.

REVIEW is never silently converted to GO. There is no path in the resolution from 'we cannot tell' back to 'ship it', and a partial report with no verdict record exits 3 rather than defaulting to anything.

The decision table

Precedence is strict: the first rule that fires decides, and NO-GO outranks REVIEW because a regression that reached significance was, for that question, powered enough.

rule 1: any judge shows a Holm-corrected significant regression -> NO-GO <-- fired on this run

rule 2: else any judge's one-sided lower bound misses the 90.0% floor and rigor does not call that sample underpowered -> NO-GO

rule 3: else any judge misses the 90.0% floor while rigor reports the sample underpowered -> REVIEW

rule 4: else any judge cannot detect a 10.0% drop at 80.0% power -> REVIEW

rule 5: else no judge regressed and every judge cleared the floor -> GO

What this report is not

This report contains no cost model, no longitudinal trend, and no claim about any item outside the golden set named in the provenance block. It compares two models on one fixed set of cases under one panel of judges, once.

Latency is descriptive only and is never a gate. A migration that is 30ms slower per call is a product decision, not a quality regression, and putting latency behind the verdict would let a faster-but-worse model pass.

Gains are shown and are never netted against flips. Two items that started working do not undo two that stopped, because the two that stopped are the ones a user will hit tomorrow.
